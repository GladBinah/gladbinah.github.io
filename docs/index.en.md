# Together Before Scripture

**성경 앞의 우리**

*Read Together, Ask Together.*

Christianity and the Bible have profoundly shaped the intellectual and ethical history of Western civilization. Whatever one's religious beliefs, reading the Bible can offer an opportunity to understand aspects of the world we inhabit and to reflect on how we live. Together Before Scripture is a space to **read the Bible, ask questions, and seek truth together**. Whether you have read Scripture for many years, have left Christianity, are questioning your faith, or are simply curious, you are welcome here.

## How we approach Scripture

I am a Christian who believes the Bible is the Word of God. That was not always the case. I once left Christianity entirely, but after various experiences I returned to reading the Bible and eventually to faith. The approach I hope to share is not one of setting aside difficult questions and insisting on belief without understanding. Like the apostle Thomas, who sought to examine the evidence for Christ's resurrection, I hope to explore what Scripture actually says by examining its language, historical context, and reasoning. My hope is that this journey may lead people toward faith in Jesus Christ.

I do not present myself as a professional theologian or Bible translator, nor do I claim that my interpretations are final. I am a Christian working in academia who wishes to share my current understanding, listen to better arguments, and revise my conclusions when the evidence warrants it.

Two projects will form the foundation of this website.

## Project 1 · Bible Translation

**An independent Korean translation of the King James Version, with documented reasoning and revisions.**

While studying the Bible and developing book-by-book commentaries, I encountered restrictions on reproducing and sharing existing Korean KJV translations, even for noncommercial purposes. This prompted me to begin a Korean translation that others can use, examine, and help improve. Both testaments will develop section by section. The first published passage is [Genesis 1:1–2:3 (0.1.0)](translation/old-testament/genesis.md).

We begin each section with the standard KJV English reading text and an independent preliminary Korean rendering. After deciding the structure, we examine the Masoretic Text for the Old Testament and the KJV, TR, and Greek textual evidence for the New Testament, comparing the existing Korean KJV editions. The 1961 Korean Revised Version is consulted only afterward for style. See our [Translation Principles](translation/principles.md).

The reasons for undertaking this translation and choosing the KJV are explained in the [Bible Translation overview](translation/index.md), with [further reading and videos](translation/references.md). The text will be divided into meaningful paragraphs, with direct speech marked using quotation marks. Each biblical book will have its own version history, while important translation decisions, objections, and revisions will be recorded.

This is not presented as a perfect or definitive translation. **Openness to review and correction is a central part of the project.**

## Project 2 · Bible Commentary

**Reading Scripture book by book, in meaningful passages, with questions and careful interpretation.**

The commentaries begin with the historical, grammatical, and literary meaning of each passage. Where warranted, they also examine connections across Scripture and theological implications. Questions and constructive criticism are welcome, and important changes in interpretation will be documented.

The commentary project will be published gradually after the translation project is under way.

## An invitation

You do not need to begin with the same beliefs or conclusions. I hope we can read Scripture seriously, respect honest questions, and discuss the evidence openly. May this be a place to discover what the Bible says, and perhaps to encounter faith in Jesus Christ for the first time—or to return to it.

**Read together, ask together.**
