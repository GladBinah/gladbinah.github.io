# Contributing and Revision Procedures

*Questions and criticism are welcome, and the sources of contributions and changes will be documented.*

## 1. What kinds of contributions are welcome?

We welcome questions and objections concerning biblical language and grammar, manuscript variants, ancient sources, historical settings, translation decisions, interpretive reasoning, and theological connections. When possible, please identify the passage or sentence at issue, the reason for your proposal, and sources that can be checked. Disagreement is welcome; not every suggestion will necessarily be adopted.

You can raise an issue through [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/GladBinah/gladbinah.github.io/issues). **Please do not publish personal contact information, details you wish to keep private, or complete copyrighted texts from others.** Matters that cannot be discussed publicly, including requests to use a legal name, require prior agreement.

## 2. Review and revision records

A suggestion may lead to a revision or a decision to retain the current wording. Significant changes will record:

- **Previous version:** The book and passage, earlier wording, document version, and a stable source link if available
- **Reader's contribution:** The objection or alternative, supporting evidence, and the name or pseudonym approved for publication
- **Review and decision:** Competing evidence, reasons for adopting or declining the proposal, revised wording, and resulting version

Typographical or formatting fixes may receive simpler changelog entries. Important objections may be preserved even when rejected, and earlier decisions can be revisited when evidence changes.

## 3. Attribution and future publication

Readers who make **substantive contributions to interpretations, arguments, or translation decisions** will be credited in the relevant revision records and, if a book is later published, in its **Contributors** section. Contributors may choose their real name, a pseudonym, or anonymous recognition. A contribution may also be linked to the specific passage or topic. Minor typographical or formatting fixes are recorded proportionately. Credit does not automatically confer coauthorship or transfer copyright.

Published GitHub history and third-party copies may be difficult to erase. If you prefer not to reveal your legal identity, use a pseudonym or request anonymous identification from the outset. We may adjust future displays when feasible but cannot promise complete erasure of previously distributed history.

## 4. Scholarly citation and exceptional permissions

**Most reader suggestions will be addressed through ordinary scholarly examination and citation.** If a reader identifies manuscript evidence, finds a weakness in an argument, or proposes an alternative interpretation, I can assess that material, credit the source and contribution, and independently revise the translation or commentary. No separate contributor contract or transfer of copyright is required for this ordinary research process. When the suggestion leads to a change, its role will be acknowledged in the revision record described above.

**Separate permission may be needed only in exceptional situations**, such as reproducing substantial portions of a reader's original translation, explanatory manuscript, or diagram beyond the scope of lawful quotation. Any subsequent commercial publication using that protected expression will also be checked for necessary rights. Permission, when needed, will ordinarily be nonexclusive and limited to the relevant use, without a blanket assignment of the contributor's copyright. Posting on GitHub Issues is not treated as consent to unrestricted republication or commercial reuse.

## 5. Example

Suppose a reader points out that a discussion of Deuteronomy 32:8 should take a Dead Sea Scroll reading more seriously. We would preserve the former text, review the reader's evidence, and, if warranted, publish a revised translation or commentary with reasons. The record would explain **whose observation prompted the change and what was changed**, with authorized attribution and, when the contribution is substantive, recognition in a future book's Contributors section.

A significant challenge need not be adopted to be worth documenting. Evidence-based criticism is part of a shared effort to understand Scripture.
