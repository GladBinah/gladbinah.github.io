# Copyright and AI Use

*Together Before Scripture — Licensing, AI Assistance, and Open Review*

Together Before Scripture distinguishes among its independent Korean Bible translation, edited English KJV reading text, and biblical commentaries. Each serves a different purpose and has its own reuse terms. The Korean translation is intended for noncommercial sharing and improvement; the commentaries remain protected against unauthorized republication while welcoming quotation, criticism, and research. These provisions apply according to the notices on individual works and applicable law; there is **no single license for the entire public repository**.

## 1. Copyright and reuse

### Korean Bible translation — CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

The independent **Korean Bible translation**, when reviewed and published, is released under **[Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/)**. Each published book and version will identify what the license covers. The license permits copying, redistribution, and adaptation for noncommercial purposes. Anyone sharing the material must provide proper credit, identify the original source and license, preserve appropriate notices about changes, and indicate modifications. A publicly shared adaptation must use the same CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license or a compatible license as allowed under the terms.

We ask users to **make the original reading and its rationale traceable**: provide the book, passage, version, and ideally a stable link, and where practical show the original and revised wording together. Reproducing the original passage verbatim alongside an adaptation is a **project recommendation, not an additional legal requirement** of the Creative Commons license. We will not impose restrictions inconsistent with its permissions.

NonCommercial use depends on the particular activity: use by a church or nonprofit organization is not automatically noncommercial for every purpose. Anyone wishing to publish commercially or use the translation outside the license may request separate permission. A rights holder may grant additional permission for their own material, but rights in protected contributions from other authors must also be cleared. Copyrighted texts consulted as references are not covered by this license.

### Biblical commentaries — All Rights Reserved

The **biblical commentaries** are independently developed interpretations and arguments. Rights in legally protectable original writing and creative editorial arrangement are reserved (**All Rights Reserved**). This is meant to prevent others from reproducing substantial passages or entire commentaries on their own websites, or republishing the work in books, ebooks, or videos that effectively replace the original, without permission. It does not seek to monopolize biblical facts, general ideas, or independently developed interpretations.

**Scholarly quotation, criticism, education, research, and independent commentary are welcome.** For example, a scholar may quote a reasonable portion of a commentary in an article, examine its arguments, and offer objections. A video creator may discuss an interpretation, compare its historical evidence, and develop an independent analysis. Please cite the title, passage, relevant version, and source whenever practicable. Applicable exceptions such as fair use and lawful quotation remain available without separate permission. Attribution alone, however, does not give permission to reproduce most of the original text or make a near-complete read-aloud that substitutes for it.

We do not claim that all text involving AI is automatically copyright-protected. Protection applies to human creative expression, selections, arrangement, and modifications to the extent recognized by applicable law. We also respect the rights of other authors and readers' lawful criticism.

### English KJV reading edition — free use and an attribution request

The **English KJV reading edition** will use a conventional standard-spelling text in the KJV 1611 translation tradition, with editorial paragraphing and quotations arranged to help readers follow the narrative and speech boundaries. This project does not claim exclusive copyright in either the original KJV English wording or its own paragraph and quotation formatting. Others may use and revise that editing. **We ask, as a matter of scholarly transparency rather than a legally imposed restriction, that users credit the project and identify the book and edition version** when following these editorial choices. Any separate existing rights affecting the underlying KJV in particular jurisdictions remain distinct.

### Scope and future editions

CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 applies only to specifically marked Korean translation texts. Unless otherwise noted, copyright is reserved in legally protectable site essays, commentaries, research records, and original explanations. Third-party sources, images, and videos retain their own conditions. A future publication may place an openly licensed Korean translation beside an All Rights Reserved commentary, with clearly separate notices; mere compilation does not automatically place the independent commentary under the translation's license. A rights holder may grant additional commercial permissions for their contributions.

## 2. AI assistance and human responsibility

This project uses **OpenAI ChatGPT extensively as a research and drafting assistant**. For durable shorthand, we describe our intended high-capability workflow as **GPT-6+ (Extra High/Pro)**. This is **our project terminology, not an official OpenAI model name**: it refers broadly to GPT-6 and, where available, more recent models operated with high-effort reasoning settings. The exact model and settings may vary; for consequential decisions we may record the model used and when the source checks took place.

The collaboration is comparable, as a working analogy, to a supervisor developing arguments with a postdoctoral researcher. I define the questions and direction; AI helps retrieve and compare historical and language sources, challenge assumptions, consider competing interpretations, and organize drafts. I then scrutinize the evidence, raise objections, request further checks, and revise the analysis. The analogy does not give an AI the qualifications, experience, accountability, or coauthorship of a human researcher.

**AI-generated suggestions, human approval, and actual verification of evidence are separate steps.** AI can misstate quotations, manuscripts, publications, or scholarly positions. Important claims will therefore be checked against accessible primary sources where feasible; unverified material will not be presented as directly verified. AI is also expected to surface evidence contrary to my preferred interpretation, and unresolved uncertainties will remain visible. I retain responsibility for research goals, textual decisions, the formal adoption of external materials, and publication. **AI involvement is disclosed, but theological, scholarly, and editorial accountability remains mine.**

### Korean lexicon translations and AI assistance

The original-language study feature draws on existing Hebrew and Greek dictionaries and morphological resources.

The translation of English dictionary entries into Korean, comparison with the original sources, and correction of those translations are delegated to OpenAI ChatGPT. Unlike the biblical translation and commentary, **I do not individually review or approve every Korean dictionary entry.** The resource is therefore not represented as a Korean lexicon independently reviewed by professional lexicographers.

When biblical research and translation reveal an inaccurate or insufficient Korean dictionary rendering, we will revisit the source entry and revise the shared lexicon where warranted.

General lexical definitions are distinguished from this project's contextual interpretations and translation judgments. Readers can consult the English source entries directly through the provided links, and significant revisions are documented.

This evolving study resource is intended to support biblical reading and research. Corrections and suggestions from readers are welcome.

### Original-language sources and licensing

Old Testament study resources draw on the [Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible Project](https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb) for WLC text, lemma and morphology data, and the [Open Scriptures Hebrew Lexicon](https://github.com/openscriptures/HebrewLexicon) for digital BDB and Strong entries. The WLC text and historical BDB and Strong dictionary texts are in the public domain; the project's additional data and dictionary XML files are licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Selected source entries are extracted and restructured, with Korean definitions and summaries and contextual notes added. The relevant edition and source links appear in the dictionary entries. These third-party terms are separate from the Korean Bible translation's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license.

## 3. Open review, revision history, and contributor credit

I do not regard the translation or commentaries as perfect at first publication. Readers may notice linguistic evidence, manuscript readings, historical sources, or weaknesses in arguments that I have missed. **Evidence-based criticism is welcome as collaboration in understanding Scripture more faithfully**, not as an attack on the project. Important objections can be recorded even when they do not ultimately change a conclusion.

When a reader's contribution leads to a revision, we aim to preserve **(a) the preceding text and version, (b) the reader's proposal and supporting reasons with their approved name or pseudonym, and (c) the revised passage and rationale**. Major interpretive changes will receive detailed records; minor spelling and formatting corrections may receive brief changelog entries. GitHub history and accompanying notes will help readers compare editions.

**Readers who make substantive contributions to a translation or commentary will be acknowledged in the Contributors section of any later published book**, using the name, pseudonym, or anonymous identification they authorize. Significant contributions can also be linked to the particular passage or argument affected. Minor corrections will be acknowledged appropriately in revision records. We collect as little personal information as necessary, recognizing that publicly replicated Git history may be difficult to erase.

**Reader feedback will ordinarily be incorporated through scholarly examination and citation.** When a reader points me toward a source, historical evidence, an alternative interpretation, or a weakness in an argument, I may assess it, acknowledge its source and the reader's contribution, and revise my own translation or commentary accordingly. As in ordinary academic research, this does not require a separate contributor agreement. **Only in exceptional cases**, such as reproducing a substantial portion of a reader's original translation, commentary, or figure beyond what lawful quotation permits, will I seek the necessary permission for that particular use, including future commercial publication where relevant. Scholarly citation and contributor credit remain distinct from the reuse of someone else's protected expression.

Feedback can be submitted through [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/GladBinah/gladbinah.github.io/issues). **Posting a public comment does not automatically transfer copyright or grant all commercial reuse rights.** See [Contributing and Revision Procedures](contributing.md) for the submission and credit process. Copyright protection against unauthorized republication does not limit thoughtful questions and criticism.


## 4. Visual Materials and Source Attribution

### Purpose of visual materials

Together Before Scripture uses paintings, historical illustrations, maps, photographs of artifacts, diagrams, and original or AI-generated images where they help readers understand biblical passages and their historical and cultural settings. These materials are intended to complement the commentaries and may also be reused in future YouTube videos introducing or discussing the relevant passages.

**Explanatory value takes priority over decoration.** Visual representations of historical events and geographical settings should be grounded, as far as possible, in biblical texts and relevant historical evidence. Uncertain details will be distinguished from established information and identified as conjectural or illustrative where appropriate.

### Use of existing artworks and images

When using existing paintings, illustrations, maps, or photographs, the project will give preference to public-domain materials and works available under suitable licenses or permissions. Even when an original artwork is in the public domain, a particular photograph or digital reproduction may be subject to separate usage conditions, depending on the jurisdiction and source. We will therefore examine the rights applicable to the actual image being used.

Where practicable, **the title, artist or creator, date, source, license or other basis for use, and any modifications** will be documented. Attribution alone does not establish permission to reuse a copyrighted work. Materials without the necessary rights will be replaced or used only after appropriate permission has been obtained. Intended reuse in both written commentaries and videos will be considered when selecting images.

### Original and AI-generated illustrations

Where existing materials are insufficient, the project may produce original maps, architectural diagrams, timelines, genealogical charts, or illustrations, including with AI assistance. These materials will be designed to reflect relevant historical and geographical evidence as faithfully as possible, with important sources identified when appropriate.

**AI-generated historical scenes and reconstructions will not be presented as authentic historical records or photographs of actual artifacts.** Details not established by Scripture or historical evidence—including personal appearances, architectural features, or precise locations—may involve artistic interpretation or conjecture. Established information and illustrative reconstruction will be distinguished, and AI involvement will be disclosed where relevant.

### Reuse in commentaries and videos

Visual materials collected or created for the commentaries will, where possible, be organized for reuse in future video productions. Records will include their sources, copyright status, scope of permission, modifications, and relevant biblical passages, enabling consistent use across different formats.

Commentaries will identify major image sources in captions or accompanying references. YouTube videos will provide appropriate attribution through on-screen credits or video descriptions as required. More detailed source information and usage records may be maintained on the website for readers and viewers who wish to examine them.

Visual materials will remain open to review and correction, just like the translations and commentaries. When new historical or archaeological evidence calls for revising a map or reconstruction, significant changes and their reasons will be documented.
