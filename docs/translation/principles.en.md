# Translation Principles — Working Draft

These principles cover both Old and New Testament translation and remain provisional; they may be revised as better evidence or working methods emerge.

## 1. Base text and reference materials

- **Old Testament textual basis:** We begin with the Hebrew Masoretic Text (MT), represented in our digital references by the [Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC)](../source-texts/wlc/index.md), with the [standard KJV 1769 English text](../source-texts/kjv1769/index.md) as a principal comparison. Where the KJV rendering materially differs from the meaning of the Masoretic Hebrew, the latter takes priority. Where the Septuagint (LXX) or Dead Sea Scrolls (DSS) provide more persuasive textual evidence for a different reading, its adoption is also considered, with alternatives and reasons documented.
- **New Testament textual basis:** We begin with the KJV English translation tradition and the Textus Receptus (TR) and Byzantine textual tradition principally associated with its translators. [Scrivener 1894 TR](../source-texts/scrivener1894/index.md) serves as a later reconstruction of the Greek readings underlying the KJV, not an edition used directly by the original translators. We distinguish the first 1611 printing from the standard-spelling 1769 tradition used in practice. Other textual traditions, including Alexandrian witnesses, are not excluded. Where variants materially affect meaning, we examine the evidence for each reading and its implications for the passage and the broader message of Scripture.
- **Korean comparisons:** Standard King James Bible (Korean), the Korean King James Bible published by Bible Believers of Korea, and the Korean King James Authorized Version (흠정역) are consulted **only after the independent draft**. After the structural decision, compare the entire section on the publishers’ official sites and document materially significant differences, actual coverage, and sources.
- **Korean style:** The dignified, traditional register of the 1961 Korean Revised Version (개역한글) is consulted **only after** an independent draft is prepared. The 1961 text is not read before drafting and is never used to decide underlying textual readings. [Book-by-book style reference](../source-texts/krv1961/index.md)
- **Greek consultation:** The Textus Receptus, particularly Stephanus 1550 as presented in Blue Letter Bible, is consulted where meaningful questions arise.

The KJV is an English translation, distinct from the Hebrew and Greek texts. In Old Testament textual criticism, individual Dead Sea Scroll readings and Greek Septuagint translations are different kinds of evidence and are not treated interchangeably. Grammar, context, and attested usage are examined where they affect an interpretive decision.

## 2. Translation and presentation

- For both testaments, begin with the KJV English text, proposed paragraph and quotation boundaries, a provisional section title, and an independent preliminary Korean rendering prepared before consulting other Korean translations. At this stage, decide only the structure and section title. Afterward, examine the substantive translation against the Masoretic Text for the Old Testament and the KJV, TR, and Greek textual evidence for the New Testament, comparing the entire section with all three Korean KJV editions. Consult the 1961 Korean Revised Version only afterward for style.
- Preserve the KJV's dignified tone while remaining intelligible to Korean readers.
- **Preserving grammatical number and meaning:** When nouns in the biblical source are morphologically singular or plural, **we generally preserve the distinction explicitly in Korean even where the result is less idiomatic**. This is especially important when number affects referent counts, scope, collectivity, or contrast. We may use plural markers or other expressions such as “several,” “each,” or “all,” while avoiding implications not supported by the source. Hebrew nouns such as **מַיִם (water)** and **שָׁמַיִם (heaven/sky)** have morphologically plural forms but need not denote separate, countable entities. Where the KJV and the source differ in apparent number, or natural Korean would conceal a material distinction, we examine the original language and context and document the translation decision in Translation Notes.
- **Clarifying potentially ambiguous Korean homonyms:** Where a Korean rendering could be materially misread because of homonyms, brief **KJV English glosses are preferred** in parentheses even in the ordinary reading text, with Hanja as an alternative when clearer. Choose the clearest minimal annotation case by case, without introducing a meaning absent from the source text.
- Organize paragraphs around literary and semantic units, preserving chapter and verse references.
- Mark direct speech with quotation marks. Where the speaker or the boundary of a quotation is disputed, document the editorial judgment.
- Treat legitimate alternatives as alternatives rather than automatically labeling one an error.

## 3. Review and versioning

- Each reading unit follows five stages: **(1) present the KJV reading edition, preliminary Korean draft, and provisional title; decide paragraphing, quotation boundaries, and title → (2) AI-led examination of the original text and complete comparison with the three Korean KJV editions, followed by a revised consolidated draft → (3) review all material translation issues together, with alternatives, evidence, recommendations, and editorial decisions → (4) AI-led integration, documentation, original-language learning resources, and final proofreading → (5) separate authorization for publication and release.** The initial structural review does not approve translation wording. Detailed comparison and revision take place after the structural decision and before the first wording review. Settled decisions are not repeatedly submitted for approval unless new material evidence warrants reconsideration.
- Each biblical book has its own text and version record; English KJV editing and Korean translation maintain separate revision histories.
- **Per-book versioning (`a.b.c`):** `a` starts at **0** and becomes **1** when the entire biblical book has been translated for the first time. `b` starts at **0** and increases by one for every newly added semantic reading section, counting cumulative sections without resetting when `a` changes to 1. `c` increases by one for a **new publication that revises existing translated text without adding a new section**. Adding a section increments `b` and resets `c` to 0. Edits to drafts **before publication** do not change `c`; they are tracked in Git history and Translation Notes. The initial completed-book version is `1.N.0`, where `N` is the total number of sections. Examples: first section `0.1.0`; second section `0.2.0`; a published correction without a new section `0.2.1`; completed book `1.N.0`. Version numbers track coverage and published revisions, **not approval or publication status**.
- **All materially significant translation differences must be documented and discussed before a decision is finalized.** When the three Korean KJV editions differ from one another or from our independent draft beyond matters of style, Translation Notes records the passage, each edition's choice in summary, alternative renderings, original-language evidence, and a recommendation. The AI presents all material issues together with evidence and recommendations. Clear editorial decisions, including batch acceptance, are incorporated without repeated approval unless a substantive objection remains. Unresolved choices remain explicitly identified; an AI recommendation alone is not approval. Record the official passages actually consulted and any limitations in coverage. Agreement among the editions does not override contrary evidence from the source text.
- When a translation choice is approved after discussion, Translation Notes records **the adopted wording, a concise rationale, relevant alternatives, and the decision date**. Agreement on an interpretive direction does not by itself approve the exact final wording. Later revisions to an already translated section retain the prior and revised readings, reasons, applicable version, and the exact publication commit reference. Changes to unpublished drafts are tracked in Git without increasing `c`.
- Constructive objections are documented, including consequential arguments that are ultimately not adopted.
- Previously published versions remain traceable through GitHub history.
