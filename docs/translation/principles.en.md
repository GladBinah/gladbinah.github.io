# Translation Principles — Working Draft

These principles cover both Old and New Testament translation and remain provisional; they may be revised as better evidence or working methods emerge.

## 1. Base text and reference materials

- **New Testament starting point:** The [KJV 1769 book-level reference text](../source-texts/kjv1769/index.md), alongside [Scrivener 1894 TR](../source-texts/scrivener1894/index.md) and the Byzantine textual tradition. The standard-spelling KJV commonly associated with 1769 is distinguished from the original 1611 printing, and significant variants are compared with other traditions, including Alexandrian witnesses.
- **Old Testament starting point:** The Masoretic Text, represented among our available digital references by the [Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC)](../source-texts/wlc/index.md), with the KJV as a major comparison translation. Substantial alternative readings reflected in the Septuagint or Dead Sea Scrolls are examined and, when appropriate, presented with alternative renderings and reasons for textual choices.
- **Korean comparisons:** Standard King James Bible (Korean), the Korean King James Bible published by Bible Believers of Korea, and the Korean King James Authorized Version (흠정역) are consulted **only after the independent draft**. Consult the publishers' official sites directly and record sources for relevant passages.
- **Korean style:** The dignified, traditional register of the 1961 Korean Revised Version (개역한글) is consulted **only after** an independent draft is prepared. The 1961 text is not read before drafting and is never used to decide underlying textual readings. [Book-by-book style reference](../source-texts/krv1961/index.md)
- **Greek consultation:** The Textus Receptus, particularly Stephanus 1550 as presented in Blue Letter Bible, is consulted where meaningful questions arise.

The KJV is an English translation, distinct from the Hebrew and Greek texts. In Old Testament textual criticism, individual Dead Sea Scroll readings and Greek Septuagint translations are different kinds of evidence and are not treated interchangeably. Grammar, context, and attested usage are examined where they affect an interpretive decision.

## 2. Translation and presentation

- Prepare New Testament Korean drafts beginning with the KJV and Old Testament drafts beginning with the Masoretic Text, **without first consulting Korean comparison translations**. The 1961 Korean Revised Version is consulted only afterward for style; Korean KJV translations are compared after drafting for meaningful differences.
- Preserve the KJV's dignified tone while remaining intelligible to Korean readers.
- **Preserving meaningful singular and plural distinctions:** When number in the biblical source text affects the number, scope, collective sense, or contrast of the referents, **we preserve that distinction as explicitly as practical in Korean**. We may use plural markers or other expressions such as “several,” “each,” or “all,” while avoiding implications not supported by the source. Hebrew nouns such as **מַיִם (water)** and **שָׁמַיִם (heaven/sky)** have morphologically plural forms but need not denote separate, countable entities. Where the KJV and the source differ in apparent number, or natural Korean would conceal a material distinction, we examine the original language and context and document the translation decision in Translation Notes.
- **Clarifying potentially ambiguous Korean homonyms:** Where a Korean rendering could be materially misread because of homonyms, brief English or Chinese-character (Hanja) glosses in parentheses may be retained even in the ordinary reading text. Choose the clearest minimal annotation case by case, without introducing a meaning absent from the source text.
- Organize paragraphs around literary and semantic units, preserving chapter and verse references.
- Mark direct speech with quotation marks. Where the speaker or the boundary of a quotation is disputed, document the editorial judgment.
- Treat legitimate alternatives as alternatives rather than automatically labeling one an error.

## 3. Review and versioning

- Each reading unit follows this sequence: **review paragraph and quotation boundaries in the KJV Reading Edition → independent first Korean draft → systematic comparison with all three existing Korean KJV translations → original-language verification, issue log, and Translation Notes → revised consolidated draft → editor's review and discussion → approval**. The comparison and resulting revisions should be completed **before the first request for the editor's review**. The 1961 Korean Revised Version is consulted for style only after independent drafting.
- Each biblical book has its own text and version record; English KJV editing and Korean translation maintain separate revision histories.
- **All materially significant translation differences must be documented and discussed before a decision is finalized.** When the three Korean KJV editions differ from one another or from our independent draft beyond matters of style, Translation Notes records the passage, each edition's choice in summary, alternative renderings, original-language evidence, and a recommendation. **Such issues remain explicitly unresolved until discussed with and decided by the editor; an AI recommendation is not approval.** Record the official passages actually consulted and any limitations in coverage. Agreement among the editions does not override contrary evidence from the source text.
- Constructive objections are documented, including consequential arguments that are ultimately not adopted.
- Previously published versions remain traceable through GitHub history.
