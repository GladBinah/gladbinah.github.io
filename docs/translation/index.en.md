## Why prepare another Korean Bible translation?

I once left Christianity entirely and later returned to faith through reading Scripture. As I prepared book-by-book commentaries, I wanted to share biblical passages alongside my explanations. But reproducing and distributing substantial portions of the **existing Korean KJV translations I had studied** involves copyright and permission restrictions, even when the intended use is noncommercial.

The **1961 Korean Revised Version (개역한글)** is an alternative whose economic copyright protection has expired in South Korea. Nevertheless, I wanted to retain the KJV tradition that had shaped my study. I therefore decided to prepare an independent Korean translation based on the KJV, consulting the traditional, dignified Korean style of 개역한글 only after producing an independent initial draft.

The goal is to make this translation freely available for noncommercial reading, sharing, and adaptation. I plan to begin primarily with the New Testament and gradually work through the Old Testament, publishing the texts together with significant translation decisions and revision histories.

## Why the KJV?

**I am not a King James Onlyist.** I do not claim that the KJV is infallible or that every other translation is mistaken. The KJV is an historically influential English Bible, first published in 1611. I value its translators' work and the tradition in which it was produced, as well as its continuity with my own study. The KJV text is also in the public domain in the United States, making it a practical source for an openly shared Korean translation. Rights in other jurisdictions require separate consideration.

My choice reflects this combination of historical confidence, continuity of study, and accessibility. The project distinguishes the original 1611 printing from the commonly used standard 1769-family text, consulting the Greek Textus Receptus when important questions arise.

I also share [historical resources and intriguing numerical observations](references.md) concerning the KJV. Certain patterns invite me to consider whether providence might have played a role beyond the translators' intentions. I do **not**, however, regard these patterns as proof of the KJV's infallibility or exclusive authority.

## Why trust Scripture despite textual variants?

The surviving Greek manuscripts of the New Testament contain real textual variants, and the Textus Receptus underlying the KJV differs in places from modern critical editions. I do not deny these differences or assume that every variant is trivial. Some variants materially affect the wording and interpretation of particular passages and deserve careful examination. Nevertheless, **I do not believe that central Christian teachings—such as the Trinity, the divinity of Jesus Christ, his atoning death, and his resurrection—depend on any one disputed reading**. These teachings are supported across multiple passages and the overall witness of the New Testament. The existence of textual variants does not, in my view, invalidate its central message.

This is why I can value and prefer the KJV **without claiming it is the only true Bible**. Other Greek editions and translations deserve serious consideration. I use the KJV as a trustworthy starting point for this project, not as an authority whose every textual and translation choice is beyond examination. See [recommended videos and textual criticism resources](references.md). Confidence in Scripture's central message is distinct from deciding which reading is original at an individual variant unit, so significant variants will still be examined on their merits.

## Old and New Testament translation

The [Old Testament](old-testament/index.md) begins with the KJV English translation tradition and the Hebrew Masoretic Text on which the KJV principally relied, while examining significant alternative readings preserved in the Septuagint and the Dead Sea Scrolls. The [New Testament](new-testament/index.md) begins with the KJV English translation tradition, the Textus Receptus, and the Byzantine textual tradition. We do not assume that every textual or translation choice is necessarily correct; significant variants will be documented along with the evidence supporting each reading and its implications for interpretation.

Work on each testament will proceed gradually, with important textual questions identified during Old Testament commentary study also preserved for later translation review. **No approved Old or New Testament translation passages have yet been published.** Each index will record book-specific versions and passage coverage as they are approved.

## Current status

The translation principles and reference Bible texts are now available on this website. The independent Korean translation and English KJV Reading Edition are still in preparation, with the New Testament as the initial priority and the Old Testament as a parallel, longer-term project.

## Process

1. Draft Old Testament passages beginning with the Masoretic Text, comparing the KJV and significant ancient textual evidence where appropriate, and prepare New Testament drafts passage by passage from the KJV.
2. Compare significant choices with existing Korean KJV translations, refining wording, tone, paragraphs, and speech boundaries.
3. Examine Masoretic, Septuagint, and Dead Sea Scroll evidence when substantial Old Testament variants arise, and consult the Greek Textus Receptus and relevant usage for New Testament questions.
4. Discuss and approve a revised passage, then incorporate it into the relevant book.
5. Document significant decisions and track book-specific revisions and public criticism.

AI supports initial drafting and research comparisons; final decisions require human review. Neither perfection nor infallibility is claimed.

## Source text references

The [KJV 1769 English reference text (66 books)](../source-texts/kjv1769/index.md) and the [Scrivener 1894 Greek New Testament (27 books)](../source-texts/scrivener1894/index.md) can be read book by book. They serve as **reference texts for comparison** and are distinct from the project's separately prepared English KJV Reading Edition and independent Korean translation. The [1961 Korean Revised Version (66 books)](../source-texts/krv1961/index.md) preserves the wording of that established translation and is consulted **only for Korean style review after completing an independent initial draft**. Other Korean KJV translations are consulted through [their publishers' official websites](../source-texts/index.md) when needed, without reproducing their texts in bulk.

## English KJV Reading Edition and language selection

We will prepare an **English KJV Reading Edition** alongside the independent Korean translation. It will preserve KJV wording while examining meaningful paragraph breaks, quotation marks for direct speech, and transitions between speakers and narration. Significant editorial decisions and alternatives will be documented.

When the first biblical books are approved, their **Korean-language pages will display the independent Korean translation, and their English-language pages will display the edited KJV reading text**. The underlying manuscripts, review decisions, editions, and revision histories remain separate. Neither reading text has yet been approved for publication.
